## CASH GAMES / GAMBLING (the categories the user asked about)

### Kegler's Cash — THIS IS THE "STRIKE JACKPOT"
Branded vendor game (© Kegler's Cash 1997-2004). The wall board with the frame grid and "red" marks tracks THIS. Full rules from the in-shop flyer (`infodocs/482199052_...n.jpg`):

- **Win up to $150.00.**
- **Buy-in is the player's choice: a quarter ($0.25) up to $5.00 per game.** (Confirms the user's ~$5 guess; the "5 tiers" = the buy-in levels.)
- **Win 10x the amount you play for.** Payout ladder shown on the flyer corners:
  - $0.25 → wins $2.50
  - $1.00 → wins $10.00
  - $2.00 → wins $20.00
  - $5.00 → wins $50.00
  - (up to the $150.00 cap across games)
- **Can play any or all games.**
- **Qualifier: "every game you get the correct marks and/or strikes based on your average group."** → It is **AVERAGE-BASED** (handicap-style), not consecutive-strike and not a red-headpin game. The earlier research's "Architecture B (average-based tiers)" was correct; the "red" on the board is the marks tracking, not a red pin.
- **Must sign up BEFORE you bowl — before the first ball of the 3rd frame of the first game.**
- Tagline: "Even if you only win once every nine games, you make a profit."

**This supersedes the speculation in `strike-jackpot-research.md`.** Keep that doc for the generic background + callback checklist, but Kegler's Cash is the confirmed answer. Remaining unknowns to confirm with owner: exact average brackets, whether All Star runs all buy-in levels, current pot/participation.

### Casino Bowling (distinct from Kegler's Cash)
- Strike turns the lane screen into a **slot machine**; spin to **win cash**. (Site audit: Mon & Thu 8PM-close, $6.25/person/game.)
- Confirmed again on the NYE Adult flyer: "Casino Style Bowling where you can Win Cash during Cosmic or New Interactive Bowling! Get a Strike and Spin the Slot Machine to Win Cash!"
- So there are TWO separate cash mechanics: Kegler's Cash (board/marks, you buy in) and Casino Bowling (slot-machine on strike).

### Not found on the page
No Monte Carlo, standalone Red Pin, or No-Tap gambling flyers surfaced in the recent photos. They may not run these. Do not assume them.
