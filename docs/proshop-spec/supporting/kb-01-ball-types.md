## The one-paragraph version (use as category intro copy)

A bowling ball is two things working together: the **coverstock** (the outer shell that touches the lane) and the **core** (the weight block hidden inside). The cover decides how much the ball grips the oil-soaked lane; the core decides how the ball rolls and revs up. The single biggest choice you make is the **coverstock family** — it sets how much your ball will hook. Everything else is fine-tuning.

---

## The four coverstock families

Order them on the site from "most forgiving / least hook" to "most aggressive / most hook." That order is also roughly beginner → advanced.

### 1. Plastic / Polyester ("spare ball")

- **What it's for:** Going straight. Shooting spares — especially the corner pins (the 7 and 10) — and learning a clean, straight delivery.
- **How it behaves:** The smoothest, slipperiest shell. Least friction, so it barely hooks. It goes where you aim it.
- **Who buys it:** Total beginners who want their *own* properly-fitted ball instead of a house ball; and *every* serious bowler, who carries a plastic ball as a dedicated spare ball.
- **Shopper takeaway:** "Straightest ball we sell. Great first ball, and the spare ball every league bowler keeps in the bag."

### 2. Urethane

- **What it's for:** Your first *controlled* hook. A predictable, smooth, arcing motion.
- **How it behaves:** More grip than plastic, but the hook is gentle and easy to read. Forgiving on the back end of the lane. Less sensitive to mistakes than reactive resin.
- **Who buys it:** Bowlers ready to move past straight but who want control over raw power; also experienced bowlers on dry or short oil patterns where a big-hooking ball would over-react.
- **Shopper takeaway:** "Your first hook ball. Smooth, predictable, hard to over-throw."

### 3. Reactive Resin

- **What it's for:** Real hook and real pin action — the modern league/tournament workhorse. This is what most "performance" balls are.
- **How it behaves:** The shell is tacky/porous and grabs the lane hard, storing energy through the oil and snapping toward the pins at the back end. Big hook potential, stronger pin carry.
- **The trade-off:** More reactive = more sensitive. It will magnify both good releases and mistakes, and it reacts more to changing lane conditions. A *beginner-friendly (entry-level)* reactive ball is the right first hook ball for most people learning to hook.
- **Three sub-types (note on the product page):**
  - **Solid** — most surface contact, earliest and smoothest read of the oil; strongest on heavier oil.
  - **Pearl** — additive in the cover makes it skid longer and snap harder/later at the back; cleaner through the fronts.
  - **Hybrid** — blend of solid and pearl; a versatile middle that many "first hook ball" recommendations land on.
- **Shopper takeaway:** "The all-arounder. Most league balls are reactive resin. Start with an entry-level solid or hybrid."

### 4. Particle (Proactive)

- **What it's for:** The heaviest oil conditions, where even reactive resin slides too much.
- **How it behaves:** Tiny particles embedded in the shell give it a microscopically bumpy surface that digs into oil for maximum traction. Most friction available.
- **Who buys it:** Intermediate-to-advanced bowlers building an "arsenal" (multiple balls for different conditions). Not a first ball.
- **Shopper takeaway:** "Heavy-oil specialist for the serious arsenal. Skip this as a first ball."

> **House-bowler note for copy:** The balls in the racks at the lanes are plastic house balls with a one-size conventional drill. A fitted plastic or entry-reactive ball of your own is the single biggest upgrade a casual bowler can make — better fit, better hook, consistent every visit.

## Coverstock — explained in one line

> **The coverstock is the ball's shell — the part that touches the lane. It controls how much friction the ball makes, which controls how much it hooks.** Smoother cover = less hook (plastic). Grippier/textured cover = more hook (reactive, particle).

Add for shoppers: the cover can be **resurfaced** at the pro shop (sanded to a duller, grabbier finish or polished to a shinier, longer-sliding one) to change a ball's reaction without buying a new ball. This is a real service worth mentioning.

---

## The core / weight block — explained in one line

> **The core is the shaped block of denser material hidden in the center of the ball. It's the engine that makes the ball rev up and roll.** Its shape decides how early the ball starts to roll and how strong/angular the motion is.

- **Symmetrical core** — smoother, more predictable, more controllable arc. Good default for most bowlers.
- **Asymmetrical core** — more aggressive, more angular, more tunable by a driller. For bowlers who know what shape they want.

You don't choose a core directly as a beginner — you choose a *ball*, and the maker pairs a cover and core to hit a described reaction. But the product page should still name the core so advanced shoppers can compare.

## The numbers: RG and Differential (the "spec block")

These two numbers appear on every performance ball box. Beginners can ignore them; intermediate shoppers compare on them. Put them in a small "specs" panel, not the headline.

### RG — Radius of Gyration
- **Plain version:** how early or late the ball wants to start rolling/revving.
- **Analogy (use it — it lands):** a figure skater spinning. Arms tucked in = spins up fast = **low RG**, revs up earlier on the lane. Arms out = spins slower = **high RG**, gets down the lane farther before revving.
- **Typical range:** about **2.46 (low) to 2.80 (high)**.
- **Rough matching:** faster ball speed pairs with **low RG** (revs earlier to compensate); slower speed pairs with **high RG** (delays the hook).

### Differential ("Diff")
- **Plain version:** how much the ball will **flare** — i.e., how much hook potential / how angular the motion can be.
- **What it really is:** the difference between the ball's low and high RG values.
- **Typical range:** **low ~0.020–0.030** (smoother, milder, more arc) to **high ~0.050–0.060** (more flare, stronger, more angular).

### How they read together (handy table for the page)

| RG | Diff | Reaction | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | High | Revs early, strong angular snap | Heavy oil, higher-rev / stronger players |
| High | Low | Delays, smooth controllable arc | Dry/short oil, higher-speed players, control over power |

> **Copy rule:** never let the numbers lead. The headline a shopper wants is "how much does it hook and how forgiving is it." RG/Diff are the fine print that backs that up.

## "Which ball is me?" — recommendation copy (drop-in)

- **Never owned a ball / I just want it to go straight:** Plastic/spare ball, fitted.
- **I want to learn to hook without it getting away from me:** Urethane, or an **entry-level reactive (solid/hybrid)**.
- **I bowl in a league and want real hook + carry:** Mid-performance reactive resin.
- **I'm building an arsenal for different oil:** Add a pearl reactive (more back-end snap) and eventually a particle (heavy oil).
- **Always end with:** "Not sure? The pro shop will watch you throw and recommend in person — no charge to ask."
