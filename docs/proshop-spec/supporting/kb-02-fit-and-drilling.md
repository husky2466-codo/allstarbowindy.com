## The big idea (use as section intro)

A bowling ball isn't finished until it's drilled to *your* hand. Two bowlers can buy the same ball and have it drilled completely differently, because their hands, spans, and releases differ. **A great ball with a bad fit throws worse than a cheap ball that fits.** That's why the pro shop exists — and why a fitting is the most valuable 30 minutes a new bowler can spend.

## What "fitting" actually measures

A pro shop tech measures and dials in several things. Beginners don't need the jargon, but the page should reassure them it's precise, not guesswork:

- **Hole size** — the finger and thumb holes are sized (and fine-tuned with inserts/tape) so they're snug but not tight. A thumb that's too tight makes you grip and hang up; too loose makes you squeeze.
- **Span** — the distance between the thumb hole and the finger holes. This is the single most important fit dimension. It must match your hand at a *relaxed* (not stretched) reach.
- **Pitch** — the angle the holes are drilled at (toward/away/left/right). Pitch controls how your fingers and thumb sit and release. Small pitch changes make a big comfort and release difference.
- **Layout / drilling** (the core position relative to your hand) — this is the advanced part that aims the ball's reaction. Beginners can ignore it; it's where an expert driller earns their keep.

A real fitting takes roughly **20–40 minutes** with a tech who measures your hand and ideally watches you throw.

## Conventional vs Fingertip grip — the one choice a shopper should understand

This is the decision most likely to come up at the counter. Explain it simply:

### Conventional grip
- **What it is:** fingers go into the holes up to the **second knuckle**; thumb all the way in.
- **Feels like:** the most secure, stable, "I won't drop it" hold. Easiest on the hand.
- **Trade-off:** less room for the fingers to rotate at release, so **less hook and fewer revs**.
- **Who it's for:** beginners, casual/recreational bowlers, kids, anyone whose priority is control and comfort. This is also how house balls are drilled.

### Fingertip grip
- **What it is:** fingers go in only to the **first knuckle**; thumb all the way in. Finger holes sit closer together, thumb a touch farther — a triangle shape that loads the fingertips.
- **Feels like:** more athletic, more demanding. The fingertips do the work.
- **Pay-off:** noticeably **more revs and more hook** — the fingers can rotate the ball at release.
- **Trade-off:** asks more of your release and your fit. A *bad* fingertip fit (wrong span or pitch) hurts more than a bad conventional fit and can cause real hand/wrist strain. So fingertip fitting must be done right.
- **Who it's for:** bowlers committed to developing a hook; most league bowlers end up here.

### The honest "should I switch?" copy
> Most house bowlers start **conventional**. Most committed league bowlers move to **fingertip**. Expect 2–4 weeks of feeling weird while your release adjusts — a temporary drop of 10–20 pins in your average is normal. Most bowlers come out the other side with more revs, better ball shape, and a *higher* average than where they started. Our pro shop will tell you honestly whether you're ready.

## Why fit matters (the section that drives people to book a fitting)

- **It protects your hand and wrist.** A wrong span or pitch makes you squeeze and grip, which causes soreness, blisters, and over time tendon strain.
- **It makes the ball *do* what it's built to do.** The reaction you paid for only shows up if your hand releases the ball cleanly — and that requires a fit that lets go at the right moment.
- **It's consistent.** A house ball is a compromise drilled for an average hand. Your own fitted ball feels the same every single throw, which is how you actually improve.
- **It's adjustable later.** Spans and reactions can be tweaked, holes can be plugged and re-drilled, surfaces resurfaced. A fitting is the start of a relationship with the shop, not a one-time transaction.
