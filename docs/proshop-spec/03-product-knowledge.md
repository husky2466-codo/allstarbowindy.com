# 03 — Product Knowledge, Shop Copy, and Glossary

This is the education/copy layer. It supplies product blurbs, fit notes, and tooltips for both shop modes. All facts below were verified; corrections from the verification pass are baked in (see callouts). Pricing lives in `02`, not here.

---

## 1. Ball types (coverstock families)

Four coverstock families, ordered least-to-most hook, mapped beginner → advanced:

1. **Plastic / polyester (spare balls).** Lowest friction, straightest path. Used for shooting spares and by absolute beginners. Least hook.
2. **Urethane.** First real controlled hook. Smooth, predictable, great on drier/shorter oil. *Note: urethane has had a major modern competitive resurgence for control on tough patterns — it is not only a "beginner/dry-lane" ball. It is also the chemical base for the reactive/hybrid/particle families.* Frame it as "control" rather than "starter-only."
3. **Reactive resin.** The mainstream hooking ball. Sub-types:
   - **Solid** — earliest, strongest, most continuous roll; reads the lane soonest.
   - **Pearl** — longer skid, sharper/later backend reaction.
   - **Hybrid** — blend of solid + pearl; the common "first hook ball."
4. **Particle / proactive.** Microscopic particles embedded in the cover for maximum traction on heavy oil. Largely legacy/niche today (most heavy-oil work is now done by strong solid reactives). Not a first ball. *Confirm whether the shop actually stocks particle SKUs before featuring this category — it may be educational-only.*

**Cover vs core (shopper framing):** the **coverstock** controls friction and how much the ball hooks; the **core** controls how the ball rolls and when it revs. Both matter, cover dominates hook.

**Core shapes:** symmetrical cores roll smoother and more predictably; asymmetrical cores are more angular and tunable.

**RG and differential (USBC-bounded):**
- **RG (radius of gyration):** 2.46–2.80 in. Low RG = revs earlier; high RG = revs later (more length down the lane). Figure-skater analogy: arms in = low RG = spins/revs sooner; arms out = high RG = later.
- **Differential:** difference between high and low RG, typically up to ~0.060 (USBC max). More diff = more track flare = more potential hook.

> **CORRECTION baked in (was an internal contradiction in the source KB):** **High RG pairs with SLOWER / lower-speed players** (and players wanting more length), NOT higher-speed players. Low RG suits faster ball speeds. Any "best for" copy must say: *High RG / low diff → dry or short oil, lower-rev / slower-speed players, control and length over power.* Do not tell a fast bowler to buy high RG.

## 2. Fit and drilling

- **Span** is the single most important fit dimension (distance between thumb and finger holes).
- **Pitch** = the angle the holes are drilled.
- **Conventional grip** = fingers inserted to the second knuckle. Easier to control, what beginners start with.
- **Fingertip grip** = fingers to the first knuckle only. More revs and hook (thumb exits first, fingers lift and rotate the ball). For developing/advanced bowlers.
- **Layout / drilling** is the advanced step that aims the ball's reaction; it is done in-store on the shop's equipment.

**Why this matters for the no-checkout model:** a ball can't just be shipped and used — it has to be drilled to the bowler's hand. This is the brand promise: *"Pick it here, we fit it to your hand in the shop."* The fitNote field turns "no online checkout" from a limitation into the reason to come in.

> **CLIENT CONFIRMATION:** the friendly "free / walk-in fitting" CTA is a policy claim, not a technical fact. Confirm the shop's actual fitting policy before publishing wording that promises free fitting.

## 3. Shoes

- **Sliding foot is opposite the throwing hand** — a right-handed bowler slides on the left foot.
- **Athletic / universal shoes:** both soles the same, slide a little, fine for casual/occasional bowlers and either hand.
- **Performance shoes:** a dedicated slide sole on one foot and a traction sole on the other, with interchangeable hook-and-loop slide pads and a brake heel. Handedness-specific.
- **Slide-number system:** lower number = less slide / more grip; higher number = more slide.
  - **Sticky or humid approaches → need a higher (more-slide) sole.**
  - **Slick / newer approaches → need a lower (less-slide) sole.**
  - Typical: S2–S5 grip range, S8–S10 slide range.
- **Slide sole materials:** microfiber / leather / felt. Traction soles: rubber. Plus a brake heel.
- Sizing varies by brand (Dexter, Storm, KR run differently). Keep the "try them at the shop" hedge; the "size down a half if between" tip is anecdotal, present it softly.

## 4. Accessories (what each is FOR)

- **Wrist supports / braces** — stabilize the wrist for a consistent release. *Most are USBC-legal, but the device cannot be adjusted during delivery and some hinged/"robotic" devices are restricted. Do not state "all braces are legal" as absolute — say "most wrist supports are USBC-legal; check the device."*
- **Rosin / grip sack** — for sweaty hands (improves thumb/finger grip).
- **Slide cream / grip cream** — manages a sticky thumb (the opposite problem).
- **Insert / thumb tape** — fine-tunes fit (fills the hole as the hand changes day to day).
- **Skin / protective tape** — prevents blisters and protects skin.
- **Kinesiology tape** — joint/muscle support.
- **Ball cleaner** — the single most impactful care product for reactive balls (see ball care below).
- **Towel / shammy** — wipes oil off the ball between shots.
- **Bags** — protect and transport balls/shoes; totes (1 ball) up through 3–6 ball rollers and tournament bags.

**Cross-sell logic for the UI:** ball → suggest cleaner + towel + bag; shoe → suggest slide sole / shoe accessories; new bowler → grip sack + tape.

## 5. Ball care (drives the cleaner cross-sell)

Reactive coverstocks **absorb lane oil** (about 1–3% of the ball's weight in the first ~20 hours of use), which kills hook over time. Cleaning and oil extraction restore reaction; resurfacing (Abralon pads) changes the surface texture/grit. This is why a ball cleaner is the highest-impact care purchase and the natural add-on to any ball.

## 6. Beginner glossary (tooltip-ready, shopper-voiced)

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| Coverstock | The outer shell of the ball; controls how much it grips and hooks. |
| Core | The weight block inside; controls how the ball rolls and revs. |
| RG | How early or late the ball starts to rev. Low = early, high = late/longer. |
| Differential | How much hook potential the core has. More = more flare = more hook. |
| Hook | How much the ball curves toward the pins. |
| Reactive resin | The standard modern coverstock that grips the lane and hooks. |
| Urethane | A smoother, more controllable cover; good on dry/short oil. |
| Plastic / spare ball | A straight ball for picking up spares. |
| Span | Distance between your thumb and finger holes — the key fit measurement. |
| Pitch | The angle a hole is drilled at. |
| Conventional grip | Fingers in to the second knuckle; easier control. |
| Fingertip grip | Fingers in to the first knuckle; more revs and hook. |
| Layout / drilling | How the holes are positioned to aim the ball's reaction. |
| Oil pattern | The oil laid on the lane; affects how much the ball hooks. |
| Slide sole | The sole on your sliding foot; swappable to match the approach. |
| Track flare | The migrating oil rings on a rolling ball; more flare = more hook. |
| Abralon | Sanding pad grit used to resurface a ball. |

For a deeper, operator-grade glossary, the **IBPSIA Glossary** (ibpsia.com/glossary-of-bowling-terms) is the industry trade-association source (~180–200 terms). Rewrite, don't scrape.

## 7. Reusable product content model (copy fields)

Every product, in either mode, should be able to populate these copy fields (the data schema in `06` mirrors them):

- `name`, `brand` (brand may be "—" / mock)
- `tagline` — one scannable line for the card.
- `blurb` — 1–2 sentences for the detail view, in shopper voice.
- `specLine` — the single most important spec for the card (e.g., "Hybrid reactive · medium oil").
- `fitNote` — the drilling/fitting note; the load-bearing field that powers the come-in-and-fit promise.
- `bestFor` — who it suits (verified RG/speed direction above).
- `careNote` — for balls, the cleaner/towel cross-sell hook.
- `glossaryTerms` — terms to surface as tooltips on this product.
