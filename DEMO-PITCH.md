# All Star Bowl — The Offer

Companion to the demo walkthrough. The walkthrough shows what's built; this is what it costs, what stays maintained, and how the relationship works. Written for a manager to relay upward to whoever signs, so every number is meant to survive being repeated secondhand without me in the room.

All dollar figures below are proposed starting points. Confirm or adjust before you print.

---

## The short version (say this first)

- The site is already built and already live. This isn't a maybe-someday mockup, it's running right now.
- It costs a fraction of what an agency charges, because it runs on Amazon's cloud (AWS) where the actual hosting bill is pennies, not the inflated monthly that website builders charge.
- You get more site than any template builder gives you, for a comparable or lower monthly than what most businesses already pay for a far simpler page.
- Two ways to do it: a clean one-time handoff (you own it, you're done), or a low monthly where I host it and stay on to keep it current. Your call, no pressure either way.

---

## Why this is cheap to run (the honest part)

Most small businesses pay a website builder (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy) somewhere around $15 to $40 a month for a basic site, and that price usually jumps after the first year.

This site runs on AWS, the same infrastructure Netflix and Airbnb use. For a site like this the actual cloud hosting cost is **under a dollar a month**. That's not a typo. Static, fast, and cheap to serve.

What that means for All Star Bowl: the price isn't padded with hosting markup. You're paying for the build and, if you want it, for someone to keep it running and current. The infrastructure underneath is genuinely a rounding error.

---

## What it's worth vs. what I'm asking

For context, not to put your books on a slide:

- A custom small-business site at this level runs **$4,000 to $8,000** from an agency, and agencies still charge **$500 to $2,500 a month** to maintain it.
- A bowling center this size is a seven-figure operation. Industry benchmarks put even a modest center around $25,000 to $30,000 per lane per year. Against a business that size, the entire annual cost of this site is less than what a couple of lanes bring in on a single busy weekend.

I'm pricing below the agency range on purpose. I'd rather have a long relationship with a real local business than win one big invoice.

---

## Option A — Build + Low Monthly (recommended)

You get the site, I host it on AWS and stay on to keep it current.

- **One-time build / setup: $1,500**
  - Final polish, your real photos and menu swapped in, your domain pointed at it, live to the public.
- **Monthly: $75 / month**
  - Hosting on AWS (handled, you never touch a server)
  - Uptime monitoring, security, backups
  - Small content changes included (hours, prices, menu items, league nights, specials, a new photo)
  - I'm your point of contact when something needs to change

Why this is the easy yes to relay upward: it's a small, predictable number, nothing to manage internally, and someone real is on the hook for it. No staff member has to learn a website.

---

## Option B — One-Time Drop Package (the floor)

If they'd rather just own it outright and not have an ongoing bill:

- **One-time: $2,500**
  - The full site, handed over, yours to keep.
  - Pointed at your domain and live.
  - A short handoff doc so whoever you use can run it.
- After that, you're on your own for hosting (still pennies on AWS) and any future changes, or you can call me ad hoc.

This is the "we just want the thing" option. It costs more up front than Option A's build fee because there's no ongoing relationship making up the difference, and because once it's handed over it's genuinely theirs.

---

## Maintenance — offered, not required

This is on the table, not a condition. Pick the relationship you actually want:

- **Hands-off (Option A):** changes go through me, included in the monthly. You email "league night moved to Thursdays," it's done same day.
- **Own it (Option B):** you take the keys. I'm available if you ever want me, but you're not locked in.
- **À la carte:** even on the drop package, I'm here for one-off updates at an hourly rate if something comes up. No retainer needed.

The point: you're never trapped. If the relationship works, great. If you'd rather own it and walk, that option exists at a fair price.

---

## Me staying on (if you want it)

I'm local, I built it, and I'd be the person keeping it current. That means:

- One person who knows the whole site, not a ticket queue.
- Changes happen fast because there's no agency layer.
- As the business changes (new specials, a remodel, a new league season), the site keeps up.

Offered because it's genuinely useful, not because you're required to take it. The drop package exists precisely so you don't have to.

---

## If they want MORE than what's built

Everything in the demo is already done. If they get excited and want to go further, these are natural next steps, quoted separately when and if there's interest:

- Real online reservations wired to your actual lane availability (right now the booking flow is a working demo; making it book real lanes is the next step)
- Live scores pulling from your actual scoring system instead of replayed demo data
- A real member/rewards system tied to your point-of-sale (so the tier discounts are automatic)
- The mobile app shown in the demo, built for real
- Online food ordering for the cafe

None of this is needed to launch. The site stands on its own today. These are "phase 2" if and when they want them.

---

## If they want LESS / aren't sure

Totally fine. The site already works as a complete marketing site without any of the integrations above. The booking, scores, and rewards pieces are honest demos that show what's possible, and they're clearly marked where they'd need to connect to your systems. Nothing is fake or hiding a gap. You can launch exactly what you saw, today, and decide on the bigger pieces later or never.

---

## The point-of-sale / restaurant / loyalty stuff

To be determined, only if it interests them. The site is built so those systems can plug in later, but none of that is required to go live, and I'm not quoting it until there's a real conversation about whether they even want it. Mentioning it as a "here's where it could go," not a line item.

---

## How to leave it with the manager

Since the manager can't say yes or no, the goal is to make the decision-maker's yes easy:

- Leave them the live URL so they can show it to whoever signs.
- Lead with Option A's numbers ($1,500 build, $75/month) because that's the easiest thing to repeat and approve.
- Make clear the drop package exists if they'd rather just own it.
- Everything bigger (real bookings, POS, app) is later and optional.

One sentence to hand them: "It's built, it's live, it runs on AWS for pennies, and it's $1,500 to launch plus $75 a month if you want me keeping it current, or a one-time $2,500 if you'd rather just own it outright."
